Touchdown. 8,000 people lose their minds. Your coach grabs you by the jersey. How did you see that? You didn't. The tip did. That's you, 3 months from now. Because one morning, you wake up and real life starts giving you tips. Like the loading screen in a video game. A little box in the corner of your eye. One line of advice every time you need it. Learn to use them and you could win any bet, ace any test, walk away from a robbery without a scratch. And that's when the tips start telling you things nobody else knows. But right now, it's the first morning and the tips are tutorial tips. Tip, open your eyes to see. Tip, toast is hot. You grab it anyway. You burn your fingers. Tip, told you. Your mom stares at you. Who are you talking to? Tip, stairs go down. You trip on the stairs anyway, all 14 of them. See, a normal person's brain can only keep track of about four things at once. Everything else slips past. The tips catch everything. Faces, footsteps, timing, then they boil it all down to one line. A normal person learns by failing over and over. You get the answer before you fail. In a video game, the people who made the game write the loading screen tips. Here, you have no idea who writes them. But there's a catch. The tips only tell you what to do. They never do it for you. You still have to be good enough to pull it off. And after the tutorial, they get good. You test them. 100 tips in 1 week, 100 right. At the arcade, your friends have spent 40 bucks on the claw machine. Nothing. Tip, this claw only grips hard every 21st try. Count. You count. Try 21. Giant teddy bear. Then the basketball game. Pros make about three out of four free throws. Tip, bend your knees. Aim for the back of the rim. You make 30 in a row. Lunch money bets? Easy. Tip, he can't throw a quarter into a cup. Bet him five. Five bucks. Tip, he always throws rock first. He throws rock. You throw paper. 20 bucks. You win 31 bets in a row. Nobody will bet you anymore. Online games are even better. 12 players left. You're hiding behind a wall. Tip, he's reloading. Push now. You push. One shot. Victory. Your friend screams so loud your mom bangs on the door. Then you try it on a skateboard. A 10-stair rail. Tip, lean forward. Keep your weight over the board. You know exactly what to do. Your body doesn't. You lean back, slip, and land on your hand. A broken wrist. That's the most common skateboard injury there is. The tip was right. You just weren't good enough yet. Six weeks in a cast, and a lot of practice. Then you land it. 10 stairs. Clean. Your friends lose it. Then the big test. Math. The one you didn't study for. Tip, don't cram. Sleep. Your brain saves what you learn while you sleep. So, you go to bed at 9:00. Your friends are up until 3:00, cramming. They walk in like zombies. During the test, you circle B. Tip, change it. Most people think their first answer is best, but studies show that when people change an answer, they usually go from wrong to right. You change it. You get a 98. The kid next to you gets a 61. Your teacher holds the paper up to the light, like it's fake. Tip, she thinks you cheated. Show your work. You walk to the board and solve the hardest problem in front of the whole class. She just nods. Then the scariest thing yet, a date. Tip, wear the blue hoodie, not the one with the stain. You're at the diner, she's across the table, your heart is pounding. Tip, your heart is at 120. It's not a heart attack, it's a crush. Tip, she's nervous, too. Tip, ask her a question, then ask another one. You ask about her dog, she talks for 20 minutes straight. Scientists tested this, people who ask more questions on first dates get more second dates. Tip, don't order the spaghetti. You order the spaghetti, sauce everywhere, all down your hoodie. She laughs so hard she snorts, then she says yes to a second date. The second date is the movies. Halfway through, her hand is right next to yours. Tip, now. You hold her hand, she doesn't let go until the credits. Walking home that night, a guy steps out of an alley. Phone, wallet, now. His hand is in his pocket. You can't see what he's holding. Your heart hits 160. Your hands shake. Fear does that. It floods your body with adrenaline and your fingers stop listening. Tip, breathe in for four, out for four. That's box breathing. Navy SEALs use it to stay calm under pressure. Your hands stop shaking. Tip, give it to him. Phones can be replaced, you can't. You hand it over, he runs. Every part of you wants to chase him. Tip, don't. Tip, look at his shoes. Red sneakers, one lace missing, a cut on his left hand. The police catch him two hours later with your phone in his pocket. How did you remember all that? I just looked. A week later, you're about to cross the street. The light is green. Everyone else steps out. Tip, wait. You stop. A truck flies through the red light, inches from your shoes. You're the kid who's never wrong. You try out for quarterback. Tip, the linebacker blinks before he blitzes. You read every blitz. Tips for everything. Tip, the bus is late today. Tip, the pizza place gives away free slices at closing. Tip, your mom is about to check your room. The news does a story on you. Your coach calls you a genius. A man in a suit offers you $10,000 a month just to tell him your tips. You say no. Little kids at the park ask you for tips. You tell them to tie their shoes. But, where it gets crazy is when the tips start reading your feelings. The other quarterback posts the spaghetti video. Everyone sees it. You want revenge. Tip, revenge feels good for about 10 minutes. Then, it feels worse. You do it anyway. You fill his car with a thousand balloons. Everyone laughs for about 10 minutes. Then, you feel sick.
