# What IF You Were The Most FAMOUS Person on Earth?

You lower your sunglasses. The guard at the arena door freezes. Behind you, the line for the championship game wraps around the block. You don't have tickets. He drops his radio. "No way, it's really you." Two minutes later, you're courtside. Your best friend just stares at you. "Dude, who are you?" Good question. Yesterday, you were nobody. Then, you woke up in a different timeline. Same bed, same face. But here, you're the most famous musician on Earth. One word from you can empty stores, fill stadiums, and move whole countries, which is exactly why some very powerful people want to own you. But right now, the arena DJ just started playing your biggest song. 20,000 people turn around. They're chanting for you to sing. Problem is, you've never heard it before. So, you grab the mic and read the words off the giant screen. The crowd sings so loud, nobody notices. See, a normal person is known by a few hundred people. Family, school, the guy at the corner store. The biggest stars in the real world have over 600 million followers. You have 2 billion. One out of every four people on Earth. A normal person posts a picture and maybe 30 people see it. You post one and half a billion people see it by lunch. A normal person walks down the street and nobody looks twice. You walk down any street on Earth and traffic stops. Whatever you touch turns into money. Whatever you say turns into news. At first, it's amazing. You visit your old school. 2,000 kids pull out their phones. The kid who called you a nobody last week asks for a selfie. Maybe later. The principal asks you to sign his old yearbook from 1992. Your best friend bets you 20 bucks you can't get a table at the busiest burger place in town on a Friday night. You walk in. They clear the whole back room and desserts on the house. He pays you. He's still not sure how. You wear plain white sneakers in one photo. By morning, they're sold out in every store in the country. A shoe company gives you your own sneaker. A game studio puts you in their biggest video game as the final boss. Another singer calls you out online, says you're overrated. You don't reply. You just drop a new song that same night. 1 hour later, yours has 10 times the plays. He deletes the post. A brand pays you $3 million for one post. That's about what the most followed person in the real world gets. At the championship, the MVP throws you the game ball. You catch it one-handed, without looking. Then, they ask you to play the halftime show. The most watched one in real life had about 130 million viewers. Yours gets 300 million. You slide across the field on your knees and stop right on the 50-yard line. Then, the world tour. 40 cities, 90,000 people a night. Night one, you stage dive. 90,000 pairs of hands. You never touch the ground. You fly in your own jet. Your best friend's first flight ever. He presses every single button. Back home, you build an arcade in your basement. Every game ever made. Pro gamers fly in just to play you. When a crowd that big screams your name, your brain floods with dopamine. The same feel-good chemical you get from winning a game, times 90,000. In one stadium, the crowd jumps so hard on the beat drop that earthquake sensors pick it up. A magnitude 2.3. That's happened for real, more than once. Then, you play a free show on a beach. 3 and 1/2 million people. As big as the biggest concert in real history. The world goes nuts. Kids everywhere copy your dance move. Pro athletes ask you for autographs. One country puts your face on a stamp. Scientists study your crowds. Here's the weird part. Scientists once found single brain cells that fire for one famous face. Only that face. Nothing else. In this world, billions of brains might have a cell just for you, and you use it. You post one link for a kid who needs surgery. $10 million in a day. But where it gets crazy is when governments notice. One real singer once told her fans to register to vote. 65,000 people signed up in a day. You have over 10 times her fans. So, a man in a dark suit comes to see you from a country you've never been to. One post. Tell your fans our leader is a great man. Name your price. You say no. A week later, your songs are banned in his country. Then thousands of fake accounts start posting your face next to his, like you said yes. Your phone gets hacked twice by two different countries. Then your own government calls. Polite, very polite. They'd like to talk before you post anything political. Then come the stalkers. When people watch you every day, their brain starts treating you like a real friend, even though you've never met. Scientists call it a one-sided relationship. Millions of people feel like they know you. Most are harmless. A few aren't. So, you hire a body double. A guy who looks just like you. 500 fans chase him to the airport while you walk out the back door. 300 fans camp outside your house every night. Some of them have been there for a month. Your security team keeps a list. 212 names. One night, a guy gets past your key. Your guards find him inside, hiding in your closet with a key to your front door. He copied it from a photo. A photo of your keychain that you posted. Researchers have done that for real, from 200 ft away. And the people around you change. Everyone laughs at your jokes, even the bad ones. Your manager agrees with everything you say. A woman says she's your long lost cousin. A man says he's your real dad. Neither one is. So, you test them. You tell three new friends three different fake secrets. The next morning, one of them is on the news. Your best friend? He still makes fun of your shoes. He's the only one. Then the internet turns on you. Someone digs up a clip 10 years old. You said something dumb. Except you don't remember it because it wasn't you. It was the other you. Someone cuts it to make it sound worse. By lunch, 100 million views and lies travel faster than the truth. Scientists checked. On social media, fake news reaches people about six times faster than true news. People who never even heard the clip want you banned from music forever. Three sponsors drop you in one day. 20 million people unfollow you. Two weeks later, the full clip comes out. It was a joke. Almost nobody shares that one. And there's a cost. You can't go to the movies or the beach or a burger place. Your mom can't buy groceries without a camera in her face. You order delivery and the driver live streams your front door. Your ears ring every night. Up front, a stadium show can hit 115 decibels. At that level, hearing damage can start in under a minute. Your body clock breaks. A new city every two days. Your brain needs about a day for every time zone you cross. You never catch up. And famous musicians die younger. One study of more than a thousand pop stars found they were up to about twice as likely to die early as normal people. You miss being bored. You miss being nobody. Some nights, you sit in a hotel room with two billion followers and nobody to call except your best friend. One year later, the biggest show of your life. 90,000 people live to a billion more. You walk out. The noise hits you like a wall. Final song. The beat drop is 10 seconds away. Fireworks loaded. Then you see it. Right at the front. The crowd is packed too tight. A girl is pinned against the barrier. She can't breathe. In a crowd crush, people get squeezed so tight they can't even lift their arms. Squeeze more than five people into one square yard and a crowd stops moving like people. It moves like water. The push from behind can top a thousand pounds, strong enough to bend steel. Nobody in the middle can stop it, not even the guards. All they can see is the people right in front of them. And when that beat drops, 90,000 people jump forward. Your manager screams in your earpiece, "Don't stop! A billion people are watching. Keep playing." 7 seconds. Stop now and you wreck the biggest moment in music history live. Keep going and the front row gets crushed. Guards can't move 90,000 people, nobody can, except the one person they're all listening to. 3 seconds. You raise one hand, the music cuts. 90,000 people go silent. Everybody, one step back. Nothing happens. One step back. 90,000 people step back at the same time, like one giant body. The front opens up. The guards lift her over the barrier. She breathes. You finish the show an hour late. The clip of you stopping it gets more views than any song you've ever made. The head of security finds you backstage. 30 years on the job, "I've never seen a crowd do that. They just needed someone to tell them." Your own government calls again. They want the clip for a crowd safety ad. You say, "Sure, that one's free." The girl is fine. She sends you a video from the hospital singing your song. Wrong words. That night your best friend sends one text, "Still hate your shoes." A week later your phone rings, unknown number. You pick up and hear your own voice. "Hey, it's me, the other you. I'm in your old bed, your old life. Nobody knows my name and honestly, best week of my life. Wait, how do I get back? Who said you're getting back?" Click.
