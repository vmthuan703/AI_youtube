The lunch room, arm wrestling, and you're losing badly. Your hand is 1 in from the table. The biggest kid in school isn't even trying. He's eating a sandwich with his other hand. Your best friend is filming. Bro, just tap out. Not yet. You blink and there it is, floating in front of your face, your stats page. Strength, 10. Speed, 10. Intelligence, 10. Charisma, 10. Luck, 10. And in the corner, 10 points unspent. One a day. You've been saving them. You tap strength once, twice, all 10. Ding! Strength, 20. Your arm stops shaking. Your hand stops sinking. Then it starts moving the other way. His sandwich drops. His face goes red. Bang! His hand hits the table so hard it cracks right down the middle. The whole lunch room goes silent. He stares at his hand, then at you, then at the two halves of the table. Best two out of three? Sure. Find another table. The lunch lady walks over, looks at the table, looks at you, writes something on her clipboard, and walks away. So, what if you could level up in real life? Here's how it works. Every night at midnight, you get one point, every day, forever. And you spend it on one of five stats: strength, speed, intelligence, charisma, luck. 10 is a normal human. Every point you add makes you 10% better than normal. So, 20? Twice as good as anyone. And the max is 999, 100 times a normal human. Your body already does this, slowly. Lift something heavy and your muscles tear a little, then spend up to 48 hours rebuilding thicker. Learn something new and the brain cells that fire together wire together. It's called adaptation. A point just does months of it overnight. But, a point can never come back out. Once it's spent, it's spent forever. And your body has to pay for every single one. So, you make a plan, one stat at a time, every single point, until it's maxed. That's 989 days each, almost 3 years. Strength first, luck last. And strength 20? It's awesome. You carry every grocery bag, one trip, pinky only. Every jar in the house is yours. Your mom stops asking your dad. The school pull-up record is 31. You stop at 100, only because the bell rang. Your best friend starts charging people to arm wrestle you. Five bucks to try. Nobody wins. You split the money. But, where it gets really crazy is when you keep going. An average guy can lift about 150 lb off the floor. The heaviest deadlift in history? About 1,100. Day 65, strength 75. You show up to a powerlifting meet in your hoodie. You lift 1,150 lb. The bar bends. The judges argue for an hour. Day 260, strength 270. A car weighs about 4,000 lb. On the way home, a car slides into a ditch, a family inside. You grab the bumper and lift. They drive away. Day 989, strength max, 15,000 lb. That's more than a full-grown elephant. But, muscle is hungry. Strongmen eat about 10,000 calories a day. You eat that by lunch. And your hands never get the memo. Phones crack. Doorknobs come off. A high five is a medical event. Your grandma asks for a hug. So, you practice on eggs first. You break 400 eggs. Then, the softest hug of her life. Strength, done. Next up, speed. The average person sprints about 15 mph. The fastest human ever, about 28. Day nine, speed 19. You're faster than anyone who has ever lived. Your gym teacher drops a stopwatch. Day 30, speed 40. 60 miles an hour. Football tryouts. The fastest 40-yard dash ever recorded is about 4.2 seconds. Yours? 1.4. The coach just hands you a jersey. But air fights back. Go twice as fast and you need eight times the power. A bug at 60 miles an hour hits like a pebble. You start wearing goggles everywhere. Day 501, speed 511. The speed of sound, about 767 miles an hour. Boom! A sonic boom rolls across town. Every window on your street shatters. The neighbors chip in for a sign, "Slow down." But speed saves lives, too. A woman collapses at the mall. Every minute without help, her odds drop about 10%. The nearest defibrillator is a mile away. You're back in nine seconds. She walks out of the hospital that week. Day 989, speed max, 1,500 miles an hour. New York to LA in under two hours. You go out for pizza in Chicago. Done. Next, intelligence. A normal person reads about 250 words a minute. Day 10, intelligence 20. Homework takes you four minutes. So, you do everyone's. Five bucks a sheet. Poker night with your friends. Chips only. 52 cards in the deck. You remember every single one. Every card that's been played, every face they make. By midnight, you own every chip on the table and three of their hoodies. Day 90, intelligence 100. You play a chess grandmaster in the park. There are more possible chess games than atoms in the universe. You've already played this one in your head. Checkmate in 19 moves. But your brain is about 2% of your body, and it burns about 20% of your energy. You get hungry thinking, and everyone else gets slow. Talking to people feels like waiting for a page to load. You finish their sentences, then their jokes. Day 989, intelligence max. You read a whole book in 4 minutes. There are math problems with a million-dollar prize for anyone who solves one. You solve one on a napkin at a diner. You leave a very big tip. Next, charisma. People decide if they like you in about a tenth of a second, before you even open your mouth. Day 20, charisma 30. You walk into a party where you know nobody. 10 minutes later, it's your party. The kid from the lunchroom, the one with the table, he asks you to be the best man at his wedding. Day 200, charisma 210. You get pulled over. You talk for 30 seconds. The officer lets you go and asks for a selfie. Day 500, a stadium, 80,000 people. At halftime, they hand you the mic. You say, "Wave." 80,000 people do the wave. Then, they do it backwards because you asked. But, you can't tell who actually likes you and who just likes your stat. Everyone agrees with everything. You tell a bad joke, 80,000 people laugh. You start to miss the word "no." Day 989, charisma max. 40 million followers. Two presidents return your calls. You say, "Nice weather." in one video, ice cream sales go up 9%. Last one, luck. The odds of winning the lottery jackpot are about 1 in 292 million. Day 10, luck 20, twice as lucky. Every light turns green. Every parking spot is right by the door. Rock, paper, scissors? You haven't lost since Tuesday. Day 90, luck 100. At a basketball game, they pick you for the half-court shot, $10,000. You don't even look over your shoulder. Swish. You bet your friends on everything. Coin flips, which raindrop wins, which fly lands first. Then they stop paying. Day 989, luck max. You drop your phone in the ocean. 3 weeks later, a fisherman finds it inside a fish, 40% battery. Every casino in Las Vegas has your photo at the door. And the jackpot? 100 times luckier is still 1 in 2.9 million. You buy one ticket, you win 20 bucks. Even luck has limits. And luck takes something, too. The half-court shot, the poker chips, the money. Was any of it you? Studies of big lottery winners find their everyday mood barely changes. Now you get it. Five stats, all maxed. The world doesn't know what to do with you. Scientists want to scan your brain. The Olympics want to test your blood. And your mom just wants you to stop emptying the fridge. And there's a cost. The page never closes. It's there when you wake up, at dinner, at funerals. Five little numbers, always floating. Your best friend is still a 10 at everything. Up in the top row, a little girl leans over the railing for a foul ball and slips. She's falling, 60 ft, about 2 seconds to the concrete. You're on the other side of the stadium.
