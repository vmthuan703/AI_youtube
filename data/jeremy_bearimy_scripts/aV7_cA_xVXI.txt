Everybody's pairing up, and your friends just bet you 20 bucks that nobody will dance with you. Go on, we'll wait. So, you look around the gym, and you see them. Thin pink threads, glowing, running from three people straight to you. One of them leads to Maya, the girl everybody says is way out of your league. You walk right past the whole gym. Use it right, and you'll never get turned down again. You'll win every bet. You'll watch the number hit millions, and one day, two threads will pull you in opposite directions, and you'll only have time to follow one. But right now, the number is three, and you've never slow danced in your life. You step on her foot twice. The number drops to two. She laughs anyway. See, a normal person has to guess who likes them, and people are terrible at it. In one study, strangers talked for 5 minutes. Afterward, almost everyone thought the other person liked them less than they really did. You don't have to guess. The number changes live. Do something cool, it goes up. Do something dumb, it drops. It's a scoreboard for being cool, and you're the only one who can see the score. And the threads pulse. When someone with a crush sees you, their heart beats faster, and their cheeks get warmer. So, their thread flickers every time. At first, it's amazing. Pick up basketball after school, girls watching from the bleachers. You hit a buzzer beater from half court. The number jumps from two to seven. You trip over the ball walking off. Six. Worth it. You land a kickflip down a seven stair in front of half the school. 19. That's real. It's called the halo effect. When people see you do one thing great, their brain decides you're better at everything. Smarter, funnier, better looking. You catch the final ball one-handed. Your team goes crazy. The number goes up by five. And there's one thread you almost don't notice. Faint. Never brighter, never dimmer. It comes from Riley, the quiet girl who sits alone in art class. It's so small, you forget about it. The number teaches you stuff, too. You make fun of a kid in class to get a laugh. It drops by four. You help the new kid when his backpack rips in the hallway. It goes up by six. That's real, too. In a study of 37 countries, the number one thing people wanted in a partner wasn't looks or money. It was kindness. Then it gets bigger. At the county fair, you take Maya on the tallest ride there.
