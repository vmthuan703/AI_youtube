You read the words above his head. Hallway, lunch break. The biggest guy in school has you pinned against the lockers. His fist is already pulled back. Half the school is watching. And above his head, in glowing letters, it says, "Cheating on his girlfriend with Jessica." His girlfriend is standing right behind him. And three lockers down, so is Jessica. So, you say it loud. "Hey, does your girlfriend know about you and Jessica?" The hallway goes dead quiet. His girlfriend turns around. Then Jessica does. "You did what? With her?" Both of them start shouting at him. He lets go of you. The whole hallway goes ooh. Your friend grabs your arm. "Dude, how did you know that?" You didn't guess. You read it. Since this morning, you can see everyone's biggest secret. One line floating above their head. The one thing they'd hate for anyone to find out. Nobody else can see it. Just you. Use it right, and you could win any bet, beat anyone at anything, catch a cheater in front of 20,000 people. And some secrets, you really don't want to know. But right now, you just used the most powerful trick on Earth to not get punched. The bully backs away with two very angry girls right behind him. And you glance at your friend. Above his head, "Still sleeps with a night light." You decide not to mention it. See, a normal person is pretty bad at catching liars. In studies, people spot a lie only about 54% of the time. That's barely better than flipping a coin. You don't need to guess. Everyone you see comes with a label, like a name tag. Except, it says the one thing they'd never write on it. The average person is hiding about 13 secrets right now. Five of them, they've never told anyone. You only see the biggest one, the top of the list. It only works with your own eyes, not on phones, not on TV, not in photos. And when someone's biggest secret changes, the words change, too, right in front of you. At first, it's amazing. Your best friend bets you 20 bucks you can't guess one thing about the gym teacher. Above the gym teacher's head, failed his driving test six times. Then you get bigger. Downtown, a guy is running a cup game on a cardboard box. Three cups, one ball, 20 bucks to play. A tourist just lost a hundred. Above the guy's head, the ball isn't under any of them. That's a real scam. It's called the shell game. The ball is hidden in his hand the whole time. So, you put down 20 bucks, and you flip all three cups at once. Empty, empty, empty. The crowd turns on him. He runs. The tourist gets his hundred back. Then your mom goes to buy a used car. $15,000. Shiny, clean. The salesman smiles. Above his head, this car was underwater in a flood. One big hurricane can flood hundreds of thousands of cars. Some get dried out, cleaned up, and sold like nothing happened. Then they rot from the inside. Hey, how deep was the water? He stops smiling. Your mom keeps her 15 grand. And the people with the biggest secrets, they're always the ones smiling the most. Then, you meet your hero, the biggest pop star on the planet, front row. She smiles right at you. Scientists found that people think about their secrets way more often than they ever have to hide them. They pop back into your head like a song you can't stop humming. That's what wears people down. And now, you're carrying everyone's. And there's a cost. You can't unsee anything. Your best friend has been your best friend since you were six. Above his head, "I'm the one who told everyone your secret in sixth grade." You never say anything, but you never look at him the same way again. And once, just to win an argument, you say someone's secret out loud. By lunch, the whole school knows. That kid doesn't come back to school for a week. You never do that again. Some secrets are kept for good reasons. Your friends plan you a surprise party for a whole month. You know the second you walk into school. You act surprised. Nobody believes you. The other team's best player is guarding you. All game, his secret has said, "Scared he's not good enough." Normal stuff. Then, the words change. "My heart keeps skipping. I never told the coach."
