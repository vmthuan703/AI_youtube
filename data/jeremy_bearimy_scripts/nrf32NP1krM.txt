# What IF You Had Psychic Powers?

The park. Pick up basketball. Five bucks a shot. And you're down 15. Your best friend is grinning. Last shot, double or nothing. You shoot. It's bad. Way short. Air ball. Then, the ball stops in midair. It hangs there, turns, floats right back over, and drops in. Swish. Nobody moves. Your best friend looks at the hoop, then at you. Did that ball just turn around? Wind. There's no wind. The ball rolls back across the court, by itself, straight into your hand. An old man on the bench drops his hot dog. He doesn't pick it up. He just slides a little further down the bench. So, we're even. So, what if you had psychic powers? Here's how it works. Your brain has 86 billion nerve cells. Every thought you have is a tiny electrical signal. The whole thing runs on about 20 watts, less than a light bulb. Scientists can already read a little of it. In 2023, a brain scanner and a computer turned brain signals into rough sentences. And psychic powers? No lab has ever measured one. Not once. Except in you. There are seven. Telekinesis, telepathy, mind reading, clairvoyance, pyrokinesis, levitation, astral projection. But, they don't come all at once. A new one switches on every 100 days. And once a power is on, it never switches off. Ever. Day one. Telekinesis. You can move things with your mind. About as much as you can lift with your arms. The TV remote comes to you. The last slice of pizza? Already yours. Your best friend's chair rolls back every time he sits down. He still hasn't figured it out. You do a kickflip. The The never touches the ground. It just waits for you. But where it gets really crazy is when it gets bigger. A fastball takes about 4/10 of a second to reach home plate. Most people can't even see it. You don't need to. At the batting cage, every pitch curves into your bat, a hundred in a row. The machine runs out of balls. Then a kid's bike rolls into the street. A car's coming. You don't move a muscle. The bike stops on the white line. But your brain pays for every push. It's 2% of your body and it burns 20% of your energy. You eat six meals a day. You're still hungry. And it's always on. Your brain reacts to a loud bang in less than a tenth of a second, before you can think. At a birthday party, a balloon pops. Every fork in the room hits the ceiling. Day 100, telepathy. Now you can send thoughts straight into other heads. In 2014, scientists sent the word hello from a brain in India to a brain in France. It took over an hour. You do it in a blink. Video games? No headset. Your whole squad hears you. They never lose a round. Your little brother gets lost at the county fair. 10,000 people. You think, "Stay by the Ferris wheel." He does. You find him eating cotton candy. The football game. You're the quarterback. You call every play in your teammates' heads. The other team can't hear a thing. 42 to nothing. But loud thoughts leak out and you can't stop them. In class, the cutest girl in school walks past. You think, "Wow." 30 heads turn around all at once. She heard it, too. She smiles. You want to disappear. Day 200, mind reading. Now it goes both ways. You hear what people think. Poker night with your friends, chips only. Every bluff, you hear it. "Please don't call. Please don't call." You call. By you own every chip on the table. Rock, paper, scissors? You haven't lost since spring. At the mall, a guy bumps into a lady, says sorry. In his head, wallet, left pocket. You follow him. One push and he trips over his own shoelaces, right in front of a cop. But scientists estimate people have about 6,000 thoughts a day. You hear everyone within 30 feet, through walls. A school hallway is a thousand voices. A stadium is a storm. And sometimes you hear what your friends really think about you. You smile, you say nothing. Then you go home and stare at the ceiling. Day 300, clairvoyance. Now you can see things before they happen, 10 seconds ahead. Your brain already does this, a little. What you see is about a tenth of a second old, so your brain guesses what comes next. Your guess is never wrong. Coin flips, free throws, which car wins at the light. You bet your friends on everything. Then they stop paying. The claw machine at the arcade? You know which grab works. You walk out with 14 stuffed animals. Then, the state championship, soccer, penalty kicks. The ball takes about half a second to reach the net. Most keepers just guess. You're in goal and you're not guessing. Left, right, left. Five shots, five saves. The whole team piles on top of you, but there are no more surprises. You see every punchline coming, every gift, every movie ending. And sometimes you see something bad, 10 seconds out, and you're too far away to stop it. Day 400, pyrokinesis. Now you can heat things up just by looking. Paper catches fire at about 450°. You can get there in a second. The bonfire won't start. You snap your fingers. Everybody cheers. Cold pizza? Hot again. Frozen car door? Open. Your friends dare you to jump in the lake in January. You heat the water around you. Hot tub. They're still on the dock, shivering. Then, a snowstorm. A hiker is stuck on the trail. Below 95°, the body starts shutting down. She's at 93. You sit beside her and turn up the heat, slowly, all night. She walks out in the morning, but you run hot all the time. And your brain starts to fail above about 104, and the heat follows your mood. A kid trips you in the hallway. You get mad. The fire alarm goes off. The whole school stands outside in the rain because of you. Day 500, levitation. Now, you can lift yourself. The world record vertical jump is about 4 ft. You take off from the free-throw line and dunk from 10 ft above the rim. The gym goes silent. Then, it explodes. You fly to school, over traffic, over everybody. A flood hits your town. A family is stuck on a roof. The water's rising. You fly over and carry them out, one at a time. But the sky has rules, too. Every 1,000 ft up, it gets about 3 and 1/2° colder. And at 18,000 ft, there's half the oxygen. Go too high and you black out. Then, you try the craziest stunt of all. You jump out of a plane, 13,000 ft, no parachute. The crowd below screams, and you land on one foot, like stepping off a curb, and you float in your sleep. You wake up on the ceiling every night. Your mom puts a seatbelt on your bed. Day 600, astral projection. The last one, and the strangest. About 1 in 10 people say they've had an out-of-body experience. Doctors have even triggered the feeling by zapping one spot of the brain. Yours is real. You lie down and float out of your own body. You can go anywhere on Earth as fast as you can think. The top of Mount Everest, the bottom of the ocean, front row at the World Cup final. A little boy goes missing in a forest. 300 people are searching. You find him in 20 minutes from your bedroom. But while you're gone, your body is empty. It can't move. It can't feel. Your mom finds you on the couch, eyes open, not answering. She thinks you're dead. You come back. She doesn't stop crying for an hour. Seven powers, all on. The world doesn't know what to do with you. Scientists want to scan your brain. The government wants you on speed dial. Casinos put your photo at the door. Game shows won't let you on. And your little brother just wants you to make him fly. And there's a cost. Your head is never quiet. Not in bed, not in the shower, not ever. Your friends stop playing cards with you. Then they stop telling you secrets. They know you already heard. And nobody can surprise you anymore. Your friends throw you a surprise party. You still had 10 seconds early. You still yell, "Whoa!" It doesn't feel the same. And every time you leave your body, it gets a little harder to come back. Five years later, you're floating a thousand feet over the mountains. Just you and the quiet. Then, a flash. 10 seconds from now. A school bus, a mountain road, ice. The brakes are gone and there's a cliff at the end. 40 kids are on board. They're singing. They have no idea. The bus is 2 miles away. Fly there? Too slow. Push it with your mind? A bus weighs 12 tons. Way too heavy. There's one way. Leave your body right now. But if you leave, your body stops floating. A thousand feet up. Nine seconds. You go. Your body drops like a rock. You're already inside the bus. The driver's hands are frozen on the wheel. You shout in his head, "Snowbank, right now!" He turns. The bus slams into the snow and stops 3 ft from the edge. You snap back. The ground is right there. You stop 1 ft above the snow. You drop into it. You lie there for a long time. Your hands won't stop shaking. 40 kids walk off that bus. Every one of them. The driver tells the news he heard a voice. Nobody believes him. A kid in the back row says she saw someone standing in the aisle. Nobody believes her, either. He's right. And you can hear what he's thinking. Thank you, whoever you are. Seven powers. And what saved them was letting go of all of them. That night, the house is quiet for once. Then, you hear a voice in your head and it isn't yours. Seven. Not bad. Most of us stop at three. You check every house on the street. Nobody's there. You've never heard a thought from this far away.
